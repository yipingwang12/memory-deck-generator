Summary: Betting the company on autonomous labs after years of losses; benches being retired.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Jason Kelly is co-founder and CEO. The company came out of MIT synthetic biology and went public by SPAC. It is the largest and longest-running synthetic biology company in Boston, and its scale is the reason it matters here even though the trend is down.

PLATFORM

The 2026 strategy is a full pivot to autonomous laboratories. Ginkgo is moving all R&D services onto Nebula, its autonomous lab in Boston, and decommissioning traditional lab benches in favour of programmable robotic infrastructure. Nebula had doubled to 105 robotic racks. Ginkgo Cloud Lab is the customer-facing product: a scientist submits a protocol in human language and gets an immediate assessment of whether the current robotic fleet can run it, plus transparent pricing.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Nebula has supported more than 100 submitted protocols, including more than 30 unique protocols written by scientists rather than automation engineers. That second number is the real proof point, because it measures whether non-specialists can drive the system.
- Building an autonomous laboratory system for Pacific Northwest National Laboratory: first 13 racks installed, expected to expand to 97 racks.
- Selected to build autonomous labs for MIT, Caltech, the University of Maryland and Northwestern University.
- Divested the Biosecurity business on 3 April 2026, retaining shareholder upside.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Public. Q1 2026 revenue $19M, down 37% year on year; adjusted EBITDA -$42M; GAAP net loss -$76M; cash $373M. FY26 cash burn guided at $125-150M. Cumulative net losses are about $6B since 2020. Cash burn fell year on year in Q1 after the divestiture. These are volatile figures and must not be carded.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not enumerated this pass. The pivot changes what the company hires for: robotic infrastructure, protocol compilation and software, rather than bench scientists. A company decommissioning benches is not a place to look for a wet-lab seat, but it may be one of the larger Boston employers of laboratory-automation software engineers.

ASSESSMENT

Read this as a turnaround bet, not a growth story. $6B of cumulative losses and a 37% revenue decline set the context, and the trend stays shrinking. But the pivot is real and specific, and the customer list is institutional rather than speculative: PNNL, MIT, Caltech, Maryland, Northwestern. If autonomous labs become the standard way experiments are run, the skill of writing protocols that compile to robots becomes valuable, and Ginkgo is the largest place in Boston to learn it. Treat it as a high-variance option and check the cash position before any conversation.
