Sector: Adjacent - neurotech implant
Location: Boston, MA
Summary: Injectable, non-surgical brain implants; explicitly aimed at disease and human augmentation.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

MIT spinout from Prof. Deblina Sarkar's Nano-Cybernetic Biotrek group (MIT Media Lab). Sarkar holds the AT&T Career Development Chair and received an NIH Director's New Innovator Award for this work. Company describes its goal as "brain-computer symbiosis beyond conventional brain-computer interfaces".

PLATFORM

"Circulatronics" — sub-cellular wireless bioelectronic devices (organic semiconducting polymer between metal layers) fused to monocytes before injection. The hybrids travel through the bloodstream, cross the intact blood-brain barrier, self-locate to a target brain region, and are powered wirelessly by near-infrared light to stimulate neurons with micron-scale precision. No surgery, which the company frames as opening implants to far more than the ~1% of patients current devices reach.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Nature Biotechnology, 5 Nov 2025 (lead author Shubham Yadav, senior author Sarkar): in vivo demonstration in mice targeting brain inflammation.
- Candidate indications named: glioblastoma, DIPG, Alzheimer's, multiple sclerosis, chronic pain; also retinal prosthesis and vision restoration (per job postings).
- Stated target: first-in-human trials within about three years of Nov 2025. Currently choosing a beach-head indication.

Actively hiring across ML, neural electrode fabrication, in vitro neural recording, retinal prosthesis, lab management and clinical/regulatory strategy. Work: pipelines over EEG, fMRI, LFP, single-spike, behavioural, clinical and blood data; models that predict brain states and personalise neuromodulation parameters. No salary listed.
