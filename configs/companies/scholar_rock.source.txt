Sector: Genomic medicine

Summary: Myostatin antibody for SMA; FDA decision was due 30 September 2026 and is unresolved here.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

A Cambridge company built on growth-factor activation biology, principally TGF-beta family signalling. The distinctive scientific idea is to target the activation step of a growth factor rather than the mature signalling molecule, which allows tissue selectivity. This entry was `carried`; it is now `verified` from company filings.

PLATFORM

Antibodies that inhibit growth-factor activation. Apitegromab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that inhibits myostatin activation, so it increases muscle mass by releasing a brake rather than by stimulating growth directly.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Apitegromab — for spinal muscular atrophy. The company calls it the first and only muscle-targeted candidate in SMA to show a statistically significant and clinically meaningful benefit in a pivotal Phase 3 trial, SAPPHIRE.
- Regulatory history matters here. The FDA first issued a Complete Response Letter that cited manufacturing observations at the Catalent Indiana fill-finish site, not clinical data. Catalent Indiana, now part of Novo Nordisk, was classified Official Action Indicated.
- Scholar Rock resubmitted the BLA using an alternate fill-finish facility, giving two independent paths to approval. The PDUFA date was 30 September 2026.
- ACTION REQUIRED: that date has passed and no outcome was found as of 14 October 2026. Check the company's investor releases before relying on this entry.
- Europe: an EMA assessment document appears to exist under the brand name Isembyld, dated 21 August 2026. That is a single indirect source and needs confirming.
- FORGE — a Phase 2 study of apitegromab in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy has started.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

A pivotal Phase 3 succeeded, the first rejection was about a contract manufacturer rather than the drug, and the company built a second fill-finish path specifically to de-risk the resubmission.
