Sector: Genomic medicine
Location: Somerville / Cambridge, MA
Summary: Taken private for about $50M and renamed; three approved gene therapies survive.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded in 1992 as Genetix Pharmaceuticals. It became bluebird bio in 2010. After the buyout it rebranded back to Genetix Biotherapeutics on 18 September 2025, returning to the original name. This file still listed it as bluebird bio, so the entry has been renamed. Sources differ on the headquarters, giving Somerville in one case and Cambridge in another. David Meek, formerly CEO of Mirati and Ipsen, became CEO on closing.

PLATFORM

Ex vivo lentiviral gene therapy for severe genetic disease. The company is one of the longest-running gene therapy efforts anywhere, and its outcome is the cautionary case for the whole modality: approvals arrived and the economics did not.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Lyfgenia (lovotibeglogene autotemcel) — sickle cell disease.
- Zynteglo (betibeglogene autotemcel) — transfusion-dependent beta-thalassaemia.
- Skysona (elivaldogene autotemcel) — cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy.
- Stated plans after the buyout: expand manufacturing capacity and improve access to the three approved therapies.
- Product sales were $84M in 2024 and $39M in Q1 2025.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Taken private by Carlyle and SK Capital Partners. The merger agreement was signed 21 February 2025 and the deal closed in June 2025. Reported value differs: about $50M in most coverage, and under $30M in some trade press. The per-share price was raised from $3.00 to $5.00 to win enough investor support. Shareholders also hold a contingent value right paying $6.84 per share if the existing portfolio reaches $600M of net sales in any trailing twelve-month period ending on or before 31 December 2027. Against $84M of 2024 sales, that threshold is a long way off.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Private and much reduced. No posting was enumerated. A company focused on manufacturing capacity and market access hires operations and commercial staff rather than researchers.

ASSESSMENT

Three approved gene therapies sold for roughly the price of a Series A. That single fact is the most useful thing in this entry, and it should inform how you read every gene-therapy company in this file, including Editas and Sarepta: regulatory success did not produce a viable business, because autologous ex vivo therapy is extremely expensive to deliver. Not an employment target. Keep it as the benchmark case, and note the name change so searches find the right company.
