Sector: Genomic medicine
Location: Cambridge, MA
Summary: Base editing, with the longest cash runway in the gene-editing tier.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded 2017 by David Liu (Broad Institute, Harvard, HHMI), Feng Zhang (Broad, CRISPR pioneer) and Keith Joung (Massachusetts General Hospital, Harvard Medical School). The same three had previously co-founded Editas Medicine. Base editing originated with two postdocs in Liu's lab, Alexis Komor and Nicole Gaudelli, who built molecular machines able to correct most known disease-related point mutations without cutting both DNA strands.

PLATFORM

Base editing — chemical conversion of one nucleotide base into another without a double-strand break. Beam was the first company formed to develop therapies on this basis. Significant ongoing investment in targeted lipid nanoparticles for delivery to haematopoietic stem cells, with candidates identified and in lead optimisation.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- BEAM-302 — alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency. First patient dosed in the global pivotal cohort, Q2 2026.
- Risto-cel — sickle cell disease, heading toward commercial launch.
- BEAM-304 — phenylketonuria, liver-targeted, clinical proof of concept pending.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Public, NASDAQ BEAM. $1.2B in cash, equivalents and marketable securities at 30 June 2026, plus an expected $200M draw from a Sixth Street facility, funding operations into mid-2029 — the most comfortable runway in this tier. Q2 2026 R&D expense $95.1M; net loss $122.7M ($1.18/share). Series A was $87M and the IPO raised $180M.
