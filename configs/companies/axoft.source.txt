Sector: Adjacent - neurotech implant
Location: Cambridge, MA
Summary: Ultra-soft implantable BCI, already in humans; best-funded Boston implant company.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded 2021 in Cambridge. Built around Fleuron, a proprietary gold-polymer composite. Paul Le Floch is the CEO. The underlying science was published in Nature in December 2023 and again in June 2025, so the material has a peer-reviewed base and not only a company claim. The company opened a subsidiary in Grenoble, France.

PLATFORM

Implantable brain-computer interface made of Fleuron, about 10,000 times softer than standard polyimide, to reduce tissue scarring and signal loss over time. The company also states that Fleuron carries 32 times more sensors per thread than existing implants. Both figures are the company's own. The device decodes brain signals. The stated clinical focus is prognosis and communication for patients with disorders of consciousness. Axoft holds FDA Breakthrough Device Designation.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- FINESSE first-in-human study: 128-channel probes placed intraoperatively; 11 patients completed across Mass General Brigham, The Panama Clinic and a hospital in China.
- First-in-human approval came at the end of 2024, so FINESSE ran about a year before the Series A.
- The Panama Clinic arm enrolled four patients during brain tumour resection.
- Next: expanded global trials and US regulatory approval.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

$55M oversubscribed Series A, announced 29-30 Apr 2026, led by C.P. Group Innovation with Alumni Ventures, the Stanford President's Venture Fund, Hillhouse Investment and Gaorong Ventures. More than $60M raised in total. Stated uses are global trial expansion, US regulatory approval, a good-manufacturing-practice plant for volume production, and team growth.

TEAMS AND ROLES

The Series A announcement names the teams the money will grow: software, microfabrication and chemistry. Software is named first, which is better evidence of a computational seat than the inference in the earlier version of this entry. No individual requisition was confirmed. A company scaling clinical trials also normally adds neural decoding, signal processing and clinical data roles.

ASSESSMENT

Real, funded and in humans, which is rare. But it is a medical-device company whose computational work is neural decoding, well away from genomics, and it augments patients rather than healthy users. Worth watching; not an obvious first application. The one genuinely encouraging detail for this file is that software is a named growth team and the company is in Cambridge. Note also that the published efficacy evidence is company-reported; no independent clinical result and no regulatory decision was found this pass.
