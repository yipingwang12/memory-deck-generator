HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Ziv Williams' laboratory at Massachusetts General Hospital. Williams is a neurosurgeon, which is what makes the work possible: recording from single neurons in a conscious human requires surgical access that almost no research group has.

PLATFORM

Neuropixels recordings of individual human neurons during language tasks, then aligned against the internal representations of language models. The question is whether brains and large language models encode meaning in comparable ways, asked at single-neuron resolution rather than by whole-brain imaging. For a methods person the appealing part is the alignment problem itself: mapping a neural population code onto a model's embedding space is a representation-learning problem.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Nature, 2024, and Nature, 2026, volume 656 — single-neuron recordings during language, aligned with language-model representations.
- Williams also co-authored the BrainGate Cell Reports paper of 30 March 2026 on decoding intended speech in locked-in syndrome. The two entries are connected through him.

ASSESSMENT

The most intellectually interesting entry in the academic group for the brain-and-AI question, and the hardest data in the file: single human neurons during language is a dataset almost nobody can produce. Two Nature papers in two years indicates a lab in strong form. The practical route is through BrainGate, where Williams co-authors, so one conversation reaches both programmes. The limits are the same as BrainGate's — neuroscience rather than genomics, academic pay per 01 — plus the structural fact that this is one principal investigator rather than an institution.
