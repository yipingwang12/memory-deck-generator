HISTORY AND PEOPLE

A Wisconsin oncology diagnostics company, best known for stool-based colorectal screening and now moving into multi-cancer blood testing. This entry was `carried` and said only that the Boston seat was unverified. That question is now settled negatively: no Massachusetts facility was found in the sources checked this pass, so treat it as remote-or-relocate.

PLATFORM

Population-scale cancer screening. The scientific content for a computational profile is classifier development on large screened cohorts, which is a different discipline from discovery genomics: the hard problems are specificity at low prevalence and real-world evidence rather than mechanism.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Cancerguard — a multi-cancer early detection blood test, launched as a laboratory-developed test at $689, with results in about two weeks.
- It began as a physician-ordered test before opening to broader consumer access through the company's own portal.
- Quest Diagnostics collects the blood draws across about 7,000 patient access locations.
- Recommended for adults aged 50 to 84 with no recent cancer history.
- The Falcon Registry real-world evidence study is enrolling up to 25,000 participants to support future regulatory submissions.
- Established colorectal screening remains the commercial base.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

ASSESSMENT

Scientifically interesting and geographically wrong. Multi-cancer early detection is one of the few areas where a genomics-trained statistician adds obvious value at scale, and the Falcon Registry is a serious evidence-generation effort. But the company is in Madison, and the Boston question is now answered in the negative rather than left open. Pursue only as a remote role. The $689 self-pay price and the Quest distribution deal are the two facts that tell you this is being built as a consumer product, which is unusual in this file.
