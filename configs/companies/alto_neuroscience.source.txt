Sector: Neurotech - precision psychiatry
Location: Los Altos, CA
Summary: Biomarker-selected psychiatry; its own trial-quality problems are the story to read.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

A public precision-psychiatry company, NASDAQ ANRO. The thesis is the most directly relevant one in this file to Shaohuan's field: use EEG, cognitive testing and wearable biomarkers to decide which patient receives which drug, instead of prescribing by diagnosis alone.

PLATFORM

Biomarker-based patient selection. EEG is the primary measurement. For ALTO-300 the company describes an EEG biomarker tracking reduced neural signal stability, which it links mechanistically to the drug's 5-HT2C activity. That is the shape precision psychiatry has to take if it is to work: a measurable brain signal tied to a specific pharmacology.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- ALTO-300 — major depressive disorder, in an EEG-defined biomarker-positive subgroup. Phase 2b. Topline was guided for mid-2026; no readout was found as of October 2026. An interim analysis covered 87 biomarker-positive patients, against a target of about 200 for the final analysis.
- ALTO-203 — anhedonic major depressive disorder, using an EEG theta/beta biomarker. A Phase 2 proof-of-concept was expected to report in Q2 2025; a later release refers to data from an exploratory study, but the efficacy result was not found.
- ALTO-100 — bipolar depression, Phase 2b ongoing. Its predecessor trial in major depressive disorder was completed and analysed.
- Further trials in schizophrenia-related cognition.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Public. No 2026 cash figure was captured this pass. For a clinical-stage company with multiple Phase 2b trials running past guided readout dates, treat the balance sheet as a question to check rather than an assumption.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not enumerated, and in California. The computational content is EEG feature engineering and biomarker validation, which is a genuine methods discipline and the closest industry analogue to computational psychiatry in this file.

ASSESSMENT

The thesis is the right one and the execution has a visible problem. In its Q1 2026 reporting Alto described instituting an enhanced sponsor eligibility review process, site-level risk scoring, and new patient and data quality requirements across the ongoing ALTO-300 and ALTO-100 Phase 2b trials — and said it did so following the full analysis of its already completed ALTO-100 trial in major depressive disorder. Read plainly, that is a company discovering data-quality or site-integrity problems in a finished trial and retrofitting controls onto the running ones. Combined with a mid-2026 topline that had not appeared by October, the picture is a delayed and remediated programme. The underlying idea remains the single best match for Shaohuan's field among industry entries, so keep the entry prominent for her, but wait for the ALTO-300 readout and read the eligibility-review language carefully before either of you engages.
