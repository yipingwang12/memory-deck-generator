Summary: The clearest academic brain-to-language-model work in Boston; decoders borrow from ASR.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

An intracortical brain-computer interface consortium directed by Leigh Hochberg, with Sydney Cash at MGH. It began as a single-institution clinical trial and grew into a multi-institutional consortium spanning MGH, Brown, Stanford, Emory and UC Davis. It is the longest-running human BCI programme anywhere, and the academic counterpart to the implant companies in this file.

PLATFORM

Intracortical recording with microelectrode arrays, decoded into intended speech or movement. The methodological point that matters here: the decoders now borrow architectures from large automatic speech recognition models. That makes this the clearest example in Boston of brain signals and language models being joined, which is the technical theme running through several entries in this dossier.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Decoding intended speech with an intracortical BCI in a person with long-standing anarthria and locked-in syndrome. Cell Reports, 30 March 2026. Co-authors include Hochberg, Cash and Ziv Williams, who has his own entry in this file.
- Inner-speech decoding in motor cortex — imagined words rather than attempted speech — reported in Cell, 2025.
- A bimanual typing neuroprosthesis was recorded in this file as Nature Neuroscience, March 2026. That citation could not be re-verified this pass; the March 2026 paper found was the Cell Reports speech study. Check the citation before quoting it.
- In 2023 the BrainGate2 consortium published in Nature that a participant with ALS generated text at a 9.1% word error rate on a 50-word vocabulary.

ASSESSMENT

The academic entry with the best claim to the brain-to-AI theme, and it is in Boston. Inner speech decoded from motor cortex is a result with no commercial equivalent in this file, and borrowing ASR architectures is exactly the kind of method transfer this profile is built on. Two honest limits. The domain is neural signal decoding, not genomics, so the CV transfers as methods rather than as subject expertise.
