Sector: Academic and non-profit
Location: Cambridge, MA
Summary: Cell-electronics hybrids that ride immune cells across the blood-brain barrier.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Deblina Sarkar's group at the MIT Media Lab, named Nano-Cybernetic Biotrek. It is the academic parent of Cahira Technologies, which has its own entry in this file. The previous row was nine words; this is now a full entry.

PLATFORM

Circulatronics. The devices are microscopic electronics about one-billionth the length of a grain of rice, built from organic semiconducting polymers layered between metals, fabricated with CMOS-compatible processes in the MIT.nano facilities. They are then integrated with living cells to make cell-electronics hybrids. The idea that distinguishes the work: rather than implanting a device, you attach electronics to a cell that already goes where you need it.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- A study in Nature Biotechnology, lead author Shubham Yadav, fused the electronics with monocytes — immune cells that migrate to sites of inflammation.
- In mice the hybrids autonomously navigated to inflammation, crossed the blood-brain barrier, and delivered electrical stimulation focused to within several microns of the target. The devices were biocompatible and did not damage surrounding neurons.
- Stated target conditions: brain cancer, Alzheimer's disease and chronic pain.
- A separate programme targets paediatric brain cancer — described as the first nonsurgical brain implant for bioelectric therapy, with nanoelectronic devices travelling through blood vessels to detect and target tumours. Highlighted by the ChadTough Defeat DIPG Foundation.
- Clinical timing is reported inconsistently: one account says the lab aims to reach clinical trials within three years through Cahira, another that human trials are several years away.

ASSESSMENT

Scientifically the most inventive entry in the academic group — using the immune system as the delivery mechanism solves the problem that defeats every implant company in this file, which is getting a device to the target without surgery. The results are in mice and the clinical timeline is unclear, so treat the three-year figure as aspiration. For this profile it is a weak direct fit and a strong thing to understand, because if circulatronics works it changes what "brain interface" means. The practical link is Cahira Technologies, which is where the commercial roles would appear.
