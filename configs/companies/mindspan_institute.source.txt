Sector: Neurotech - brain mapping (nonprofit)
Location: Cambridge, MA
Summary: Boyden and Kording's new institute to map and simulate human brain molecules and wiring.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Nonprofit founded 2026. Co-CEOs Ed Boyden (MIT; optogenetics, expansion microscopy) and Konrad Kording (UPenn; ML and data-driven neuroscience, C. elegans simulation).

PLATFORM

Expansion microscopy at scale for molecularly annotated tissue; paired physiology (voltage imaging, optogenetics, patch clamp) on the same tissue; segmentation of terabyte image volumes; and a proposed ML "ultrastructure-to-dynamics compiler" that predicts neural physiology from anatomy (arXiv 2603.25713, Mar 2026).

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Stated goal: tools to analyse the human brain, shared openly.
- Boyden's stated longer aim is biologically accurate whole-brain simulation, starting with small brains.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Undisclosed. No funding announcement was found this pass. Open Philanthropy has given an unrestricted gift to Boyden's MIT lab for brain-mapping work, covering materials, equipment and salaries for up to nine postdoctoral researchers, staff scientists and graduate students over two years. That gift went to the MIT lab and not to Mindspan, so it is background and not institute funding. An undisclosed balance sheet is the main risk in this entry: a 2026 nonprofit with no published runway cannot be assessed for stability.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Founding-team hiring, all on-site in Cambridge: research scientists in expansion microscopy engineering, optical physiology, electrophysiology and computational pipelines. The Computational Pipelines role (3D segmentation, PyTorch, distributed processing) pays $120,000-$159,000. Postings name four internal teams: Scalable Chemistry, Microscopy and Optics, Computation and Analysis, and Optical Physiology and Neurotechnology. Hiring runs through a Rippling applicant-tracking page rather than an institutional careers portal, which is normal for an institute in its first year.

ASSESSMENT

See 13_Boyden_Mindspan.md. About a 50-60% match to the posted computational role; the molecular/omics side of the programme is the better latent fit. One of the four named teams is Computation and Analysis, so the dry-lab side is a standing team and not one post. Weigh that against the undisclosed funding.
