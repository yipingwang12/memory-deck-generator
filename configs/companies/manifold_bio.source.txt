Summary: Protein barcodes that let one mouse run a hundred in vivo tests at once.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

George Church is the founder. Gleb Kuznetsov and Pierce Ogden co-founded the company in May 2020 and moved it from the Wyss Institute to Harvard's Pagliuca Life Lab. Kuznetsov is the CEO. Shane Lofgren leads business development. The company is one of several Church spinouts in this file, so the lineage runs back to the same lab as E11 Bio's scientific advice.

PLATFORM

M-Codes, a protein barcoding method. Each protein design carries a unique peptide barcode. That lets a mixture of designs be tracked quantitatively as it moves through a living mammalian system. The practical effect is multiplexing: up to about 100 simultaneous tests in a single mouse. The company describes the work as AI-guided protein design plus massively multiplexed in vivo screening, and says the screens exist to build the datasets that train its own computational models. That loop is the reason this entry rates fit 2 rather than 1: the assay is designed to produce training data, not just answers.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Platform company rather than a clinical pipeline. No disclosed clinical asset.
- 2026: a joint study with NVIDIA validating Proteina-Complexa, testing 1M binder designs against 127 targets in one experiment.
- The stated focus has shifted over time, from oncology applications in early coverage to tissue-targeted medicines.
- The underlying multiplexed protein binding and barcoding method is published through the Wyss Institute's technology portfolio.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

$5.4M seed in 2020 and a $40M Series A led by Triatomic Capital. Later investors include Section 32, FPV Ventures, Horizons Ventures and Tencent. Totals disagree across aggregators: one gives $46.4M across four rounds and another $58.8M. The most recent listed round is from October 2025 and is labelled non-equity assistance, which usually means a grant or an award rather than a priced round. Read that as a company that has not raised a priced round since the Series A.

TEAMS AND ROLES

One aggregator listed 13 open positions. A posted Scientist role in phage display and discovery describes the AI-guided design and multiplexed screening work directly. The hiring shape is therefore wet-lab screening with a computational consumer downstream, not a dry-lab-first organisation.

ASSESSMENT

The best science-to-profile match in the promoted AI-native group, because the platform exists to generate training data and the company says so openly. Two cautions. It has not raised a priced round since the Series A, so the balance sheet is the open question. And the NVIDIA study is a validation collaboration, not revenue. Worth a conversation, and the Church lineage gives a warm-introduction route through the Boston network.
