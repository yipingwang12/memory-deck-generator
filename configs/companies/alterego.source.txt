Sector: Neurotech - neuromuscular interface
Location: Boston, MA
Summary: Silent-speech wearable for querying AI without speaking aloud.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

AlterEgo grew out of a 2018 MIT Media Lab prototype. Arnav Kapur built it as a graduate student in the Fluid Interfaces group, which Pattie Maes leads, and Maes supervised the project. Kapur spun the research out as a company and is its CEO. Max Newlon is co-founder and COO. Newlon earlier grew a neurotech startup that started in the Harvard Innovation Labs. The company unveiled its wearable in September 2025 in an Axios exclusive. Earlier passes gave 2025 as the founding year and April 2025 as the date the IP moved from MIT. This pass did not confirm either date, so no card uses them. Some listings give the legal name as AlterEgo AI Inc.

PLATFORM

Sensors on the face and neck pick up the faint neuromuscular signals sent to the speech muscles when a person speaks internally. Machine learning decodes them into words. Replies come back through bone conduction, so only the wearer hears them. The company says the device registers only intentional motor signals and does not try to read thoughts. It reads muscles, not the brain. An earlier pass recorded the name "Silent Sense" for this technology. No source found this pass uses that name, so no card uses it. Reports put the accuracy of the 2018 MIT prototype at 92 percent.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Silent typing and AI queries, real-time translation, private conversation.
- Speech restoration for people with impaired speech.
- No shipping date disclosed as of November 2026.
- The company showed the device publicly at the Axios AI+ Summit in Washington DC on 17 September 2025.

ASSESSMENT

The most literal "talk to an LLM without speaking" company in Boston, and the neuromuscular channel is the one that works on healthy users today (compare Meta's wristband). But nothing about its funding or staff is public, so due diligence comes before any application. Two further cautions. Coverage around the 2025 unveiling described the prototype as bulky and wired, with no close-up product images released. And the technology traces to a 2018 MIT Media Lab prototype, so the core idea is eight years old while the company is one year old. That gap is normal for a Media Lab spinout, but it means the hard part is productisation and not the decoding science.
