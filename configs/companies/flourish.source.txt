Sector: Neurotech - brain-inspired AI
Location: New York, NY
Summary: $500M at $2.5B to copy the brain's algorithm; neuroscience for AI, not AI for neuroscience.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded by Thomas Reardon and Rob Williams, and based in New York — this file previously had the location as unconfirmed. Reardon's history is unusual twice over: he led the Internet Explorer team at Microsoft, then co-founded CTRL-labs, which Meta acquired for a reported $500M to $1B. CTRL-labs became Meta's Neural Band, which has its own entry in this file, so Reardon appears twice in this dossier on opposite sides of the same idea. Rob Williams is a former Amazon executive. The company has recruited the longtime DeepMind researcher Greg Wayne.

PLATFORM

Brain-inspired artificial intelligence, built around a system referred to internally as Cortex AI. The distinguishing target is power: 20 to 50 watts, roughly a laptop or a couple of LED bulbs, against the megawatts that current frontier training consumes. Reported components include memory systems modelled on the hippocampus and models capable of continuous learning rather than train-then-freeze. Note the direction of travel carefully: this is neuroscience used to build better AI, not AI used to understand brains. That is the opposite of almost every other entry in this group.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- No commercial product. The work is research-stage.
- Reports suggest possible deployment on low-power devices and partnerships with major chip manufacturers.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

$500M at a $2.5B valuation, closed in early June 2026. Backers include Jeff Bezos, Lux Capital, Alphabet's GV and Catalio. Accounts of who led differ: one says Lux and GV were expected to lead per Bloomberg, another credits Bezos. Bezos is reported to have started at $50M and then nearly doubled that stake. This is the second-largest raise of any entry in the neurotech group, behind only Neuralink.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Little public hiring information. The one named recruit is Greg Wayne from DeepMind, which tells you the hiring bar and the discipline: this is a frontier-AI research organisation that happens to take its inspiration from neuroscience. Expect ML research roles rather than biology roles.

ASSESSMENT

Enormously well funded and only loosely relevant. The fit 2 rating is generous: a computational biology profile is not what a brain-inspired AI lab hires, because the work is architecture and learning rules rather than biological data analysis. There is no biological dataset here to bring methods to. The reason to keep the entry is the Reardon connection, which links it to Meta's Neural Band, and as a market signal: $500M at $2.5B for a pre-product research company shows where the capital in this field is actually going — into AI that borrows from brains, not into reading them. New York also fails the Boston filter.
