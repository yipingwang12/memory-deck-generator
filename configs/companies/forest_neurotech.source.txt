Sector: Neurotech - ultrasound BCI (nonprofit; now Arbor)
Location: Los Angeles, CA
Summary: Nonprofit ultrasound BCI; its researchers seeded Merge Labs.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded 2023 in Los Angeles as a Focused Research Organization (FRO), a nonprofit startup form for science, under Convergent Research (Schmidt Futures network). In March 2024 Convergent Research announced a new $14M commitment from Eric Schmidt. Tyson Aflalo and Sumner Norman, later Merge Labs co-founders, worked on its ultrasound technology, and Merge Labs was spun out of Forest in 2025. The organisation now recruits as Arbor, "the new name for Forest Neurotech", still a Convergent Research FRO. Some listings use Arbor Neuroscience. No official announcement of the rename was found.

PLATFORM

Minimally invasive ultrasound BCI to sense and modulate whole-brain activity without penetrating the brain. In October 2023 Butterfly Network announced a five-year co-development agreement with Forest for brain-computer interfaces built on its Ultrasound-on-Chip technology. The device is called Forest 1. It is a wearable ultrasound system that both measures and stimulates brain activity. Arbor's job postings compare it to a wearable functional MRI with higher resolution. It places no electrodes inside the brain.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- A UK study is the main human programme. ARIA funds it with about £6.5M. Partners: Barking, Havering and Redbridge University Hospitals NHS Trust, the University of Plymouth, and Forest.
- It is a single-centre exploratory study of functional ultrasound imaging of brain states through a craniectomy window. The Trust sponsors it, and Aimun Jamjoom, a consultant neurosurgeon there, leads it.
- Participants have skull defects from a craniectomy. The reason is physical: ultrasound does not pass through an intact skull well. ARIA's announcement said up to 30 participants; a later Trust update reported 18 of a planned 20 recruited.
- Aims: safety and tolerability, and how brain regions work together across mood and emotional states. Later work adds focused ultrasound neuromodulation to the imaging.
- Forest has said it hopes to run a full trial in a condition such as depression afterwards.
- Reported risks: ultrasound energy can heat tissue, and reporting also raises possible unintended changes in decision-making or personality.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Philanthropic via Convergent Research: $14M committed by Eric Schmidt (March 2024). The ARIA award of about £6.5M for the UK study is separate.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Small research team. No posting was found. The UK trial is run with NHS and university partners, so clinical staffing sits mostly with them and not with Forest.

ASSESSMENT

Mislisted as Cambridge in some directories. Its main value to this file has changed: it is not only Merge Labs' origin, it is Merge Labs' parent. Merge is a spinoff of Forest, and Forest continues independently while collaborating. So Forest is the nonprofit research arm of the approach and Merge is the funded commercial arm. Forest is also further along in humans than Merge: it has a live NHS trial while Merge has no human device. A nonprofit in Los Angeles is not an employment target here, but it is the place to watch the science.
