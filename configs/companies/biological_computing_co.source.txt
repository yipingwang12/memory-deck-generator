Sector: Neurotech - biological computing
Location: San Francisco, CA (Mission Bay)
Summary: Encodes data into living neurons and reads the responses into AI models.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded by the neurosurgeon-scientists Alex Ksendzovsky, CEO, and Jon Pomeraniec, COO. The lead investor spells the CEO's name Ksendzovsky; some reports use Ksendsovsky. The company was formerly called Biological Black Box. With its seed round it opened a flagship lab in Mission Bay, San Francisco. The founding year is given as 2022 here; the investor says only that the research progressed in 2021 before the company started. Re-verified in October 2026 against the lead investor's announcement and trade coverage, so confidence returns to `verified`.

PLATFORM

Data is encoded into living neurons, the neurons' responses are recorded, and those responses are mapped onto AI models. The company has claimed a text-to-video model about 80% cheaper and five times faster as a result. Treat that claim with scepticism: it is a company figure, the mechanism connecting cultured neurons to a generative video model is not publicly explained, and no independent benchmark was found.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Wet lab plus machine learning. No product was identified.
- An AWS partnership was recorded in September 2026.
- Do not confuse this company with Cortical Labs, a Melbourne company in the same space that sells a $35,000 commercial biological computer, the CL1, and has deployed units at the National University of Singapore and in a Melbourne data centre. Cortical Labs is listed in this file's `gaps` block as a name surfaced but not researched.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

A $25M seed led by Primary, February 2026, joined by Builders VC, Refactor Capital, Wonder Ventures, E1 Ventures, Proximity and Tusk Ventures. No later round was found.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not enumerated. The structure — a wet lab feeding an ML pipeline — is unusual and would suit someone comfortable on both sides, which is a genuine match to this profile in principle.

ASSESSMENT

The most speculative entry in the neurotech group, and the one to treat most carefully. Neurons-in-the-loop computing is a real research area, but the specific commercial claim here, an 80% cheaper text-to-video model, is the kind of statement that needs independent verification before it means anything. The field also has a credibility history: Cortical Labs was criticised by more than thirty neuroscientists for claiming its earlier system possessed sentience. The company facts are now verified; the performance claims are not. San Francisco also rules it out on geography.
