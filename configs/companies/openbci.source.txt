Sector: Neurotech - open-source biosensing hardware
Location: Brooklyn, NY
Summary: Open-source EEG boards and the Galea multi-sensor headset.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Started by Conor Russomanno and Joel Murphy, his professor at Parsons. One account says the work grew from Murphy's DARPA-funded low-cost EEG project. Brooklyn lab. The first Kickstarter ran late 2013 and raised more than $200,000 (one source: $215,438 on a $100,000 goal).

PLATFORM

Low-cost open-source EEG boards used widely by researchers and hobbyists. Galea: a headset that combines EEG, EMG, EDA, PPG and eye tracking in one device, built for VR and mixed reality.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Varjo partnership, 31 May 2022: Galea integrated with the Varjo Aero headset.
- Galea Beta began shipping pre-orders in August 2024 (one source).
- Pupil Labs collaboration, 1 October 2025: Galea with Neon eye tracking.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Kickstarter-funded at start; no venture round was verified.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not researched. Small hardware company.

ASSESSMENT

Infrastructure for the noninvasive research community rather than a product company in the therapy race. Galea is the multimodal bet: many weak signals combined rather than one strong one. Small; New York.

KEY FACTS

- OpenBCI was started by Conor Russomanno and Joel Murphy of Parsons
- OpenBCI's first Kickstarter, late 2013, raised more than $200,000
- Galea combines EEG, EMG, EDA, PPG and eye tracking in one headset
- Galea was integrated with the Varjo Aero headset (2022)
- October 2025: OpenBCI and Pupil Labs combined Galea with Neon eye tracking
