HISTORY AND OWNERSHIP

Foundation Medicine was founded in 2010 by Todd Golub, Levi Garraway and Eric Lander.

At the time of founding, Eric Lander was director of the Broad Institute and a professor at
Harvard Medical School and MIT, Todd Golub was founding director of the Broad Institute's
Cancer Program at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, and Levi Garraway was an assistant professor
of medicine in medical oncology at Dana-Farber.

The founding lineage is therefore Broad Institute and Dana-Farber, the same network the Izar
and Azizi connections already run through.

Roche acquired Foundation Medicine in 2018 and has operated it as a subsidiary since.

Before the Roche acquisition the company had raised approximately $96.5 million across four
rounds from thirteen investors.

Daniel Malarek has been chief executive officer since 2023.

WHAT IT DOES

Foundation Medicine performs comprehensive genomic profiling of tumours to guide targeted
therapy selection and clinical trial matching.

In practice that is clinical next-generation sequencing interpretation carried out at
commercial scale.

PRODUCTS

FoundationOne CDx is the company's comprehensive genomic profiling test and companion
diagnostic, providing information for five tumour types: ovarian, lung, breast, colorectal
and melanoma.

FoundationOne Heme is the assay for haematologic malignancies and sarcomas.

The liquid biopsy line has been sold as FoundationACT and later as FoundationOne Liquid.

In April 2026 Roche entered into a definitive merger agreement to acquire SAGA Diagnostics,
whose tumour-informed molecular residual disease platform will be fully integrated into
Foundation Medicine once the transaction closes.

The SAGA transaction was expected to close in the third quarter of 2026 at the latest.

Molecular residual disease testing detects cancer remaining after treatment, and adds
longitudinal monitoring to a portfolio that was previously built around profiling a tumour
once.

PEOPLE AND TEAMS

Garrett Frampton is vice president and lead of the Computational Discovery group within
research and development.

The Computational Discovery group conducts early-stage research on cancer genomics,
computational pathology and clinical outcomes for patients.

The Computational Biology team is an interdisciplinary group comprised of computational
biologists, molecular biologists, software engineers and other skilled specialists who work
to enhance understanding of next-generation sequencing.

LOCATION AND SCALE

Foundation Medicine is headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and posts senior
computational biologist roles in the Boston area.
