Sector: Neurotech - imaging tools
Location: Cambridge, MA
Summary: Commercial arm of expansion microscopy, aimed at diagnostics.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Founded 2015 in Cambridge, MA by Ed Boyden and Jeremy Wertheimer to commercialise expansion microscopy. Wertheimer previously founded ITA Software. Boyden's MIT team (Fei Chen, Paul Tillberg and Boyden) first described expansion microscopy in 2015. A Wilhelm Exner Foundation profile names Expansion Technologies and Cognito Therapeutics as Boyden's start-ups. The company website is extbio.com.

PLATFORM

Expansion microscopy for clinical diagnostic and predictive applications. The underlying method swells a fixed tissue sample with a water-absorbing polymer. The sample grows about fourfold, so nanoscale features become visible on an ordinary microscope. The clinical form is expansion pathology (ExPath), published in Nature Biotechnology in 2017 by Yongxin Zhao, Octavian Bucur, Andrew Beck, Boyden and colleagues. ExPath processes most types of clinical sample used in pathology, including formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens. The academic method keeps moving: Boyden's lab published expansion-revealing microscopy in 2022, which adds the fluorescent markers after expansion instead of before, and ultrastructural membrane expansion microscopy in Nature Communications in 2025, which makes cell membranes visible. Boyden co-authored a review of the whole family of methods in Nature Reviews Methods Primers in 2026.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Nanoscale imaging for pathology; no disclosed product launch.
- One known limit of the method: amyloid-beta plaques in Alzheimer's disease still cannot be resolved.
