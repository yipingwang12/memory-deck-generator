Sector: Adjacent - cellular reprogramming
Location: Boston, MA
Summary: First partial-reprogramming therapy in humans; its efficacy readout is methylation.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

A Boston company built on partial epigenetic reprogramming: expressing reprogramming factors transiently to restore a younger cell state without erasing cell identity. Arthur Benjamin is associated with the company's public communication on the programme.

PLATFORM

Controlled, transient expression of three transcription factors — OCT4, SOX2 and KLF4, together called OSK. These are three of the four Yamanaka factors, with MYC deliberately omitted. Full expression would drive cells back to a pluripotent state and risk tumours; partial, controlled expression is intended to reset the epigenome while the cell stays what it is. Delivery is by intravitreal injection, so the eye is the first target organ because it is small, enclosed and directly measurable.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- ER-100 — the lead programme, for optic neuropathies. Indications are open-angle glaucoma and non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy.
- First participant dosed, announced 9 June 2026. Phase 1 enrols up to 12 participants across two dose levels, with five years of follow-up.
- Reported as the first cellular rejuvenation therapy using partial epigenetic reprogramming to receive FDA clearance to enter human trials.
- Preclinical basis: non-human primate studies showing controlled OSK expression, restoration of methylation patterns, and improved visual function.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

An $80M Series D closed 8 April 2026, funding operations into the second half of 2027. That runway comfortably covers the Phase 1 but not the five-year follow-up, so expect further financing to be necessary.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not enumerated this pass. The relevant observation is what the preclinical readout was: restoration of methylation patterns. Measuring that means DNA methylation arrays or sequencing and epigenetic-clock analysis, which is computational epigenomics and sits directly beside the CV. Whether the company staffs that internally or buys it was not established.

ASSESSMENT

07 read this as a gene-therapy company whose core sidelines the ML edge, and for the clinical programme that is right — an intravitreal AAV therapy is virology and ophthalmology. But the old row missed the part that matters here. The scientific claim of partial reprogramming is an epigenomic claim, and it is validated by methylation data. Any company whose primary efficacy evidence is a methylation pattern needs people who can analyse methylation at scale. That makes the latent fit better than fit 1 suggests, even though the advertised work is not. It is also in Boston, which few adjacent-field entries are. Ask specifically how they measure reprogramming before accepting 07's rating.
