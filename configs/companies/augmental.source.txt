Sector: Neurotech - assistive interface
Location: San Francisco, CA
Summary: Tongue-controlled mouth touchpad; MIT Media Lab origin. Now on general sale in the US.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Launched late 2019 by Tomás Vega (MIT; co-founder and CEO) and Corten Singer (co-founder and engineer), who met at UC Berkeley. The MouthPad grew out of MIT Media Lab work. Early funding came from MIT's E14 Fund; lead seed investor Baukunst. The company used MIT support including the Venture Mentoring Service and the MIT I-Corps programme.

PLATFORM

MouthPad: a custom-fit intraoral device on the roof of the mouth that works as a hands-free touchpad, mainly for people with limited hand control. The user drives a cursor with the tongue. No surgery is involved. Each unit is made from a 3D scan of the user's upper jaw, taken at a dental practice.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- MouthPad was first shown publicly at CES 2024 and then sold through an Early Access programme.
- MouthPad went on public sale across the US on 22 July 2026. Sales are US-only; no European date has been set.
- More than 100 people with mobility challenges use it, and the waitlist is over 6,300.
- Vox, a skin-worn microphone pendant that picks up sound through the body as well as the air, launched in beta alongside it at $200. Augmental calls the pair an ecosystem of wearables called Skinware.
- One user reached 10.47 bits per second in a cursor-control task. The company says this matches current state-of-the-art implanted BCIs without surgery. That is a company claim and not an independent result.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Seed; investors include Baukunst, E14, Eclipse, Costanoa, Berkeley SkyDeck. An MIT Media Lab article put the total at just over $1M, but that predates the 2026 launch. No 2026 round was found, so the company appears to be funding itself partly through sales.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Small; hardware and product. No posting was enumerated this pass. A general-sale launch normally adds support, fulfilment and clinical-ops roles rather than research roles.

ASSESSMENT

An input device, not a neural interface, and not in Boston. But "logged for completeness" is no longer accurate, and the fit 0 and flat ratings now understate it. The product shipped to the general US market in July 2026, it has a 6,300-person waitlist, and a second product followed. Treat the bits-per-second claim with caution, because the comparison to implanted BCIs is the company's own and the task is narrow. The reason to keep this entry is as a benchmark, not as an employer: it shows how much usable bandwidth a non-surgical interface can deliver, which is the number every implant company in this file is implicitly competing against.
