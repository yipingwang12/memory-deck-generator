Sector: Academic and non-profit
Location: Boston, MA
Summary: Clinical genomics plus precision psychiatry. Relevant to both careers.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

The Center for Genomic Medicine (CGM) belongs to Massachusetts General Hospital. It houses the Psychiatric and Neurodevelopmental Genetics Unit. Michael Talkowski directs the centre. He is also Associate Professor of Neurology at Harvard Medical School. The centre calls itself one of the largest hubs of genomic medicine research in the world. It has 46 faculty. All 46 hold Harvard Medical School appointments. Many also work at the Broad Institute. That double appointment is the structural point: CGM and the Broad are one talent pool, not two.

PLATFORM

Clinical and population genomics, variant interpretation, and psychiatric genetics. The centre groups its work into four areas. Populations to Variants catalogues human genetic variation without regard to disease. Variants to Diseases and Traits links variants to disease. Variants to Function and Mechanism asks how a variant changes biology. Variants to Diagnosis and Treatment turns the findings into care and new therapeutics. The fourth area is the closest match to the current DGG work.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Jordan Smoller directs the Psychiatric and Neurodevelopmental Genetics Unit and the Center for Precision Psychiatry; Professor of Psychiatry at Harvard Medical School.
- Heidi Rehm — clinical genomics and variant interpretation, a direct analogue of the current DGG work. She is Chief Genomics Officer in the MGH Department of Medicine. She is reported to lead the centre's Genomic Medicine Unit. She is also medical director of the Broad's clinical research sequencing platform.
- Michael Talkowski — structural variation; his lab is joint between MGH CGM and the Broad and was hiring a Computational Scientist this pass. He also directs the centre.
- Mass General Brigham runs a large institutional program on responsible AI in care delivery.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

NIH and institutional. The Healthcare Training Program (T32) in Precision and Genomic Medicine, co-led by Smoller and Rehm and supported by NHGRI, funds two years with salary and travel. Faculty also hold their own NIH grants, so each lab hires on its own money. There is no single central hiring budget to watch.

TEAMS AND ROLES

The Talkowski lab Computational Scientist role is the concrete opening. The T32 is a funded training route but would be a step back in title and pay. Apply to a named lab, not to the centre. A faculty post in neurogenetics was also open in the Department of Neurology this pass, which shows the centre recruits at faculty level too.

ASSESSMENT

Rehm's clinical-variant work is the closest institutional analogue to DGG anywhere in Boston. Smoller is the other credible Shaohuan anchor alongside the Stanley Center. One point is worth the attention: Talkowski both directs the centre and runs the structural-variant lab that posted the most on-target job found this pass. The same name therefore appears in the Broad entry and here. Treat the two entries as one target with two front doors.
