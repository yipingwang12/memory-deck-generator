Sector: Neurotech - closed-loop deep brain stimulation
Location: Minneapolis, MN (operational HQ)
Summary: First commercial adaptive DBS; the largest installed base of sensing brain implants.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Medtronic DBS therapy for tremor received FDA approval in 1997 (Activa). One aggregator gives 2002 for the Parkinson's indication. This entry covers the DBS business only, not Medtronic.

PLATFORM

Percept neurostimulators with BrainSense: the implanted DBS lead records local field potentials (LFPs) while it stimulates, in clinic and at home. Beta-band oscillations in the LFP track Parkinson's motor symptoms. BrainSense Adaptive DBS (aDBS) uses that signal to adjust stimulation in real time, which makes it a closed-loop BCI therapy.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Percept PC: FDA approval 24 June 2020 (supplement P960009/S361). Third-party sources call it the first sensing DBS system.
- Percept RC (rechargeable): FDA approval 8 January 2024; battery life at least 15 years; CE mark December 2023.
- BrainSense Adaptive DBS and Electrode Identifier: EU/UK CE mark 13 January 2025. Martijn Beudel did the first programming in Amsterdam.
- FDA approval 24 February 2025. Medtronic calls it the world's first adaptive DBS system. Parkinson's only.
- Basis: ADAPT-PD, an international randomized crossover study of 68 people with Parkinson's already on DBS, comparing dual- and single-threshold aDBS with continuous DBS.
- Existing Percept patients can receive aDBS. Medtronic calls it the largest commercial BCI launch, citing more than 40,000 Percept users (company claim, volatile count).

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Not applicable: a division of Medtronic plc.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not researched. Neuromodulation R&D sits in Minneapolis; signal-processing and algorithm roles exist for sensing and aDBS.

ASSESSMENT

The quiet giant of closed-loop neurotech. While startups chase first implants, Medtronic turned an installed base of tens of thousands of DBS patients into a sensing BCI platform by software and firmware. The beta-band biomarker is the reason aDBS works in Parkinson's and not yet elsewhere. Large-company stability, Minneapolis.

KEY FACTS

- Medtronic DBS for tremor: FDA approval 1997
- Percept PC, the first sensing DBS neurostimulator, FDA approval June 2020
- BrainSense records local field potentials through the DBS lead during stimulation
- Beta-band oscillations are the Parkinson's signal used by adaptive DBS
- Percept RC rechargeable: FDA approval January 2024, battery life at least 15 years
- Adaptive DBS CE mark: 13 January 2025
- Adaptive DBS FDA approval: 24 February 2025, for Parkinson's
- ADAPT-PD: randomized crossover study of 68 people with Parkinson's
- Electrode Identifier was approved together with adaptive DBS
- The first adaptive DBS programming was done in Amsterdam by Martijn Beudel
