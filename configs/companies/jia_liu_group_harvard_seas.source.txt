Sector: Academic and non-profit
Location: Allston / Cambridge, MA
Summary: The one lab where BCI hardware meets single-cell genomics. The CV is the qualification.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Jia Liu's group at Harvard School of Engineering and Applied Sciences. Liu trained in Charles Lieber's lab, where the injectable flexible mesh nanoelectronics approach originated, and was named to MIT Technology Review's 35 Innovators Under 35. The lineage matters because the mesh-electronics idea is now about fifteen years old and this group is its current frontier.

PLATFORM

Two things at once, and the combination is what makes this entry exceptional. First, soft mesh nanoelectronics for long-term brain interfaces — electronics compliant enough to sit in tissue for months without scarring. Second, in situ electro-sequencing: chronic electrophysiology integrated with three-dimensional single-cell spatial transcriptomics, and analysed with deep learning to link genes to electrical activity cell by cell. That second capability is the only place found in this entire dossier where brain-interface hardware and single-cell genomics are the same experiment.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Cyborg organoids: nanoelectronic sensors embedded in developing organoids, recording electrophysiological activity for 90 days. Published in Nano Letters with Liu as senior author, covered by SEAS in April 2026.
- In situ electro-sequencing, combining chronic recording with spatial transcriptomics.
- The organoid work gives a rare view of early development, because the sensors are embedded as the tissue forms rather than inserted afterwards.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Academic. The lab lists an NSF Emerging Frontiers in Research and Innovation award dated 18 November 2024, tied to the cyborg brain organoid work. EFRI awards are multi-year, so that grant should still be live. A student in the group, Tianshu Tan, was selected as a 2026 NSF Graduate Research Fellow, which is a reasonable proxy for a group in good standing.

TEAMS AND ROLES

A postdoctoral posting from December 2025 sought a nanofabrication specialist for designing and fabricating nanoelectronics and flexible electronics. Note what that reveals: the advertised need is hardware, not computation. The electro-sequencing side implies computational need, but no such posting was observed. That is the gap to probe directly — ask who analyses the spatial transcriptomics.

ASSESSMENT

The single best scientific fit in the dossier, and it is in Allston. Everywhere else in the neurotech field the CV is a partial match requiring a domain rebuild; here chronic electrophysiology is combined with 3D single-cell spatial transcriptomics, which is the exact existing skill set applied to a new question. The honest caveats are that it is one principal-investigator lab rather than an institution, academic pay applies per 01, and the only observed posting was for a nanofabrication specialist. The right move is a direct approach to Liu about the computational side of electro-sequencing rather than waiting for a posting, because the posting that matches may never appear.
