HISTORY AND PEOPLE

This is the largest psychiatric genomics group in the world. It sits inside the Broad Institute. The Stanley Family Foundation funds it. Ted and Vada Stanley gave the first money in 2007. Ted Stanley first offered $10M, then raised the offer to $100M over ten years. Edward Scolnick was the founding director. Steven Hyman became director in February 2012 and held the post until 2024. Hyman ran the National Institute of Mental Health from 1996 to 2001. He was then Provost of Harvard from 2001 to 2011. He now directs the Broad's Program in Brain Health. Morgan Sheng is Co-Director and Head of Therapeutics. He joined in 2019 from Genentech, where he was Vice-President of Neuroscience from 2008 to 2019. The current sole director was not confirmed from outside this pass.

PLATFORM

Psychiatric genomics at scale. The work has three parts: statistical genetics, single-cell brain genomics, and the translation of genetic findings into biological mechanism. Sheng's side adds a therapeutics arm, so the centre does not stop at gene discovery.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Steven McCarroll — single-cell brain genomics. The closest match to Shaohuan's omics training.
- Benjamin Neale — psychiatric statistical genetics.
- Morgan Sheng — molecular and neurobiological mechanisms of schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, and therapeutics for them.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

The Stanley Family Foundation renewed its commitment with $280M, announced March 2026 — the largest single funding event in this file, and the strongest possible signal of stability in a field where 02 records that NIH and NIMH cuts closed off the U-M search. An earlier commitment of $650M came in 2014. That was the largest gift ever made for psychiatric research at the time. The centre therefore has two very large commitments behind it, twelve years apart.
