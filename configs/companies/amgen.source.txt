HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Amgen was founded on 8 April 1980 in Thousand Oaks, California, as Applied Molecular Genetics. It took the name Amgen in 1983. Its headquarters are still in Thousand Oaks. Robert Bradway has been CEO since 2012 and Chairman since 2013. Amgen lists 360 Binney Street in Cambridge as one of its US locations. A 2017 company release said Amgen had invested about $100M in the Cambridge facility since 2015, with more than 400 staff based there. The site's historical focus was neuroscience.

PLATFORM

Broad biologics portfolio. Amgen acquired deCODE genetics, the Reykjavik human genetics company, in December 2012, for $415M in cash. Amgen said deCODE would help it find and validate human disease targets. No Cambridge-specific platform or computational unit was identified this pass.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Otezla: Amgen completed its acquisition of Otezla from Celgene on 21 November 2019, for $13.4B in cash. Celgene had to sell it as a condition of its merger with Bristol Myers Squibb.
- Horizon Therapeutics: Amgen completed this acquisition on 6 October 2023, for about $27.8B. It brought three rare inflammatory disease medicines: Tepezza, Krystexxa and Uplizna.
- ChemoCentryx: Amgen agreed on 3 August 2022 to acquire it for about $4B, to add to its autoimmune strategy.

TEAMS AND ROLES

In 2019 Amgen exited neuroscience research. It gave Massachusetts notice of 149 layoffs at its Cambridge operation, effective at the end of 2019. Amgen said it would consolidate its US research primarily in Thousand Oaks and San Francisco, and keep an operations and process development presence in Massachusetts. This dates the report that earlier passes could not place. Amgen also confirmed about 40 job cuts in July 2026 tied to an internal reorganisation, without naming functions or locations, and a 22-person cut at its Horizon unit in Rockville, Maryland in March 2026.
