Summary: Chemoproteomics against transporters; first oral PKU candidate in the clinic.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

A Boston chemoproteomics company. Its chosen target class is solute carrier transporters, a family long considered hard to drug with small molecules. The scientific claim of the company is the screening method rather than any single molecule.

PLATFORM

Chemoproteomics applied to solute carrier transporters. The platform screens for compounds that bind these membrane transporters, which conventional screening handles poorly. Success against one SLC target is the evidence that the method works.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- JNT-517 — lead asset. An oral allosteric inhibitor of SLC6A19 for phenylketonuria. It is the first small-molecule SLC6A19 inhibitor to enter clinical development for PKU.
- Mechanism: SLC6A19 is the main transporter that reabsorbs phenylalanine in the kidney and intestine. Inhibiting it makes the body excrete phenylalanine in urine instead of reabsorbing it, which lowers plasma levels. That is a different strategy from restricting diet or replacing the missing enzyme.
- Preclinical: inhibition raised urinary phenylalanine excretion and lowered plasma levels in a mouse PKU model.
- Phase 1 in healthy volunteers: safe and well tolerated, and it increased urinary phenylalanine excretion. Safety was the primary objective, with pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics secondary.
- The work is published in the Journal of Clinical Investigation.

TEAMS AND ROLES

ASSESSMENT

A clean scientific story: a new screening method, applied to a target class that resisted small molecules, producing the first oral candidate for a disease currently managed by diet. Published in a strong journal, which is better evidence than most entries in this group carry. The limits for this profile are real though. The domain is proteomics and rare metabolic disease, not genomics, and nothing public has happened since the Phase 1. Low priority, but upgrade the entry from `single` to `verified` and keep it visible: a positive Phase 2 would change the picture quickly.
