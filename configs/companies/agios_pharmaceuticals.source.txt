Sector: Genomic medicine
Location: Cambridge, MA
Summary: Rare-disease company with a launched drug, two approvals pending and $965M in cash.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

A Cambridge company originally built on cancer metabolism, which divested its oncology business and rebuilt around rare haematology. The scientific through-line is metabolic enzymes: its drugs activate pyruvate kinase, the enzyme that limits energy supply in red blood cells. This entry was `carried` and unverified in this file; it is now `verified` from the company's 2026 quarterly reporting.

PLATFORM

Pyruvate kinase activation. The same mechanism serves several red-cell diseases, which is why one molecule supports multiple indications and multiple launches.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Mitapivat — marketed. Approved in pyruvate kinase deficiency, and launched in thalassaemia as AQVESME in late January 2026.
- Sickle cell disease: the FDA granted the mitapivat sNDA priority review, with a PDUFA goal date of 1 November 2026. That date is imminent and is the main event in this entry.
- Tebapivat — a next-generation PK activator, with two Phase 2 readouts flagged for 2026.
- AG-236 — moved into Phase 2/3 for polycythaemia vera.
- Cevidoplenib — licensed in for immune thrombocytopenia, with a $25M upfront payment.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

Public, NASDAQ AGIO. Cash and marketable securities were $964.8M at 30 June 2026, which is among the strongest balance sheets of any mid-size company in this file. Q2 2026 mitapivat revenue was $44.7M, of which $40.9M was US, against $12.5M in the US in Q2 2025 — the jump reflects the thalassaemia launch. Full-year US PK deficiency revenue guidance was reaffirmed at about $45-50M. Q2 R&D was $100.8M against $91.9M, and SG&A $61.5M against $45.9M, the rises driven by the cevidoplenib upfront and launch spending.

Not enumerated this pass. A company running a commercial launch, a pending approval and three clinical programmes is hiring, and the mix will favour clinical development, medical affairs and commercial over discovery informatics.

ASSESSMENT

Quietly one of the healthier entries in this file: revenue growing more than threefold year on year in the US, nearly $1B of cash, and a second approval decision due on 1 November 2026. The fit limit is honest — this is enzymology and clinical development, not genomics, so it applies methods rather than growing the ML-build edge. But for a stable Cambridge employer with money and momentum in a market full of contractions, it deserves more attention than its former eight-word row implied. Check the 1 November PDUFA outcome before approaching.
