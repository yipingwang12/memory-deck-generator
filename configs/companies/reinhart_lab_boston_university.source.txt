Location: Boston, MA
Summary: The most direct academic memory-enhancement result in Boston, and it has a live 2026 grant.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

Robert Reinhart's laboratory at Boston University. Reinhart won the 2022 grand prize of the Science and PINS Prize for Neuromodulation for personalising noninvasive treatment of cognitive disorders, including age-related memory decline.

PLATFORM

High-definition transcranial alternating current stimulation, applied to specific oscillatory couplings rather than to brain regions generally. A small alternating current is used to synchronise activity rhythmically between prefrontal and temporal areas. The mechanism is measured, not assumed: improvements were linked to restored theta-gamma coupling in EEG.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- The landmark study, Nature Neuroscience 2022, used 42 younger and 42 older adults and improved working and long-term memory in older adults for at least a month. Benefits appeared to extend to younger participants too.
- A live NIH grant, 1R01AG097619-01, is listed for fiscal year 2026, funded by the National Institute on Aging at $343,350. Its stated aim is to identify optimal tACS protocols and build an adaptive system that adjusts stimulation to each person's neural connectivity.
- That grant is the finding: personalised, closed-loop stimulation is a modelling problem, so the lab's direction is moving toward computation.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

NIH, through the National Institute on Aging. The $343,350 award for fiscal 2026 is modest in absolute terms but it is dated, specific and current, which is more than most academic entries in this file could show.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not enumerated. But read the grant text: building an adaptive system that adjusts stimulation to individual neural connectivity requires per-subject modelling of EEG. That is a methods role in substance, even if it is not advertised as one.

ASSESSMENT

Upgraded in usefulness by the grant. The 2022 result — memory improvement in older adults sustained for a month, with a measured mechanism — remains the most direct academic enhancement finding in Boston, and the fiscal 2026 award shows the work continuing toward closed-loop personalisation. Compare Elemind in this file, which is the commercial version of the same closed-loop idea in sleep. The limits are real: this is neuroscience and signal processing rather than genomics, the lab is small, and the award is modest. But it is in Boston, it is funded now, and the direction of travel is computational.
