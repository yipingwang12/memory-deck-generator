Sector: Neurotech - implant BCI hardware
Location: Salt Lake City, UT
Summary: Maker of the Utah array, the electrode under most human intracortical BCI research.

HISTORY AND PEOPLE

The Utah array comes from Richard Normann's lab at the University of Utah, late 1980s to mid-1990s. Blackrock Microsystems bought Cyberkinetics, the BrainGate company that held the array. Sources give the purchase as 2008 or early 2009. One source names Marcus Gerhardt as co-founder with Normann; one source says the Blackrock Neurotech brand dates from 2021.

PLATFORM

The Utah array (sold as the NeuroPort Electrode): a small grid of silicon needle electrodes that goes into the cortex and records single neurons. It has been in human implants since 2004. The NeuroPort recording system has FDA clearance for short-term recording. Most academic BCI results of the last 20 years, including BrainGate, used this array.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- BrainGate: the first participant, Matt Nagle, received a Utah array (company timeline).
- 2016: a participant with a Utah array implant fist-bumped President Obama with a robotic arm.
- MoveAgain: a home-use BCI system built on the Utah array. FDA Breakthrough Device designation, November 2021. It lets people with paralysis control a mouse, keyboard, tablet, wheelchair or prosthetic by neural activity.
- No FDA market approval for MoveAgain was found as of November 2026. One aggregator reports a June 2026 Breakthrough designation for a high-density array; not confirmed.

FUNDING AND PARTNERS

April 2024: Tether, the stablecoin company, invested $200M through Tether Evo and became the majority stakeholder. The money goes to commercialisation. No valuation was disclosed.

TEAMS AND ROLES

Not researched. Hardware, neural-engineering and clinical roles in Salt Lake City.

ASSESSMENT

The supplier under the field rather than a front-runner in it. Its array is the reference that Neuralink, Paradromics and Precision define themselves against: more channels, less tissue damage, wireless. The Tether money makes it well funded, but no product approval has followed. Utah location; no Boston route.
