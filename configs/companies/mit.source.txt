PLATFORM

The relevant unit is Manolis Kellis's group, which he heads as the MIT Computational Biology group within the Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science. Its stated scope is disease circuitry, genetics, genomics, epigenomics, coding genes, non-coding RNAs, regulatory genomics and comparative genomics, applied across Alzheimer's disease, obesity, schizophrenia, cardiac disorders, cancer and immune disorders.

PIPELINE AND PRODUCTS

- Scale: the Kellis group reports transcriptional and epigenomic maps covering 823 human tissues, 1,500 individuals and 7.5 million single cells. That is the largest single-cell resource named anywhere in this dossier.
- A headline finding: Alzheimer's variants act primarily through immune processes rather than neuronal ones. Note how that connects to the Gene Lay Institute entry — immunology is where the neurodegeneration genetics is pointing.
- The Tsai-Kellis collaboration spans statistical genetics, computational genomics, epigenomics, machine learning and single-cell profiling across brain diseases. 07 flags Kellis as a rare two-career lab, meaning it could support both careers in one place.
- Also here: Alex Shalek, methods plus immunology, from the Regev lineage; Ed Boyden, who has his own entry via the Synthetic Neurobiology Group; and Li-Huei Tsai, who co-founded Cognito Therapeutics.
- A SuperUROP project under Kellis studied brain aging using single-cell sequencing of gene expression and chromatin accessibility in neurons across ages.
